Panthera

Lions

Lions are the only truly social big cats, living in prides of a single male lion and multiple female lions, plus their cubs. The females to most of the hunting, working together as a team to take down larger prey, while the males defend the pride's territory. Male lions also have large, distinctive manes.

Tigers

Tigers are the biggest of the big cats, living in the jungle and enjoying swimming in the water, unlike most cats. They have distinctive vertical stripes that functions as natural camoflage. They are primarily solo ambush hunters.

Learn more!

Leopards

Leopards have the widest range of all the big cats, found across Africa and Asia. They are powerful climbers and often carry their prey up into trees to keep it away from other predators. They have a distinctive pattern of black dots. There are also so-called black panthers, leopards with dark black fur, although their spots can still be seen if you look closely.

Learn more!

Jaguars

Jaguars are the largest cat native to the Americas, ranging from Mexico all the way into South America. They have an incredibly strong bite, able to pierce thick turtle shells and crush skulls. Like tigers, jaguars enjoy the water and often swim in lakes and rivers. They have a spotted pattern like leopards, but with their own distinctive style of spots.

Learn more!

Clouded Leopards

Clouded leopards are elusive cats native to South-East Asia and the Himalayan highlands. Despite their smaller size, they are related to other members of the Panthera family. They have distinctive cloud shaped pattern on their coats. They have the largest canines relative to their size of any living cat species, making them a spiritual descendant of the fearsome sabre-tooth tiger. They are exellent tree climbers and leapers. Despite their name, they are not closely related to leopards.
